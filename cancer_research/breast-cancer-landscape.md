## 1. The three receptors

Nearly all clinical classification of breast cancer rests on three proteins measured on the tumor tissue itself.

| Receptor | What it is | How it's measured | Positive threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ER** (estrogen receptor) | Nuclear hormone receptor; estrogen binding drives proliferation | IHC, % of nuclei stained | **≥1%** nuclear staining (ASCO/CAP). 1–10% is reported as "ER-low positive" — a distinct, less endocrine-responsive group |
| **PR** (progesterone receptor) | Nuclear hormone receptor; an ER-pathway output, so it partly reads as a marker that ER signaling is intact | IHC, % of nuclei stained | **≥1%** nuclear staining |
| **HER2** (ERBB2) | Growth factor receptor on the cell surface; amplification drives aggressive growth | IHC 0/1+/2+/3+, then ISH (FISH) if IHC 2+ | **IHC 3+**, or **IHC 2+ with ISH amplification** |

ER and PR together are usually collapsed into **HR (hormone receptor) status** — HR+ means ER+ *and/or* PR+.

**Why the thresholds matter.** These are consensus cutoffs, not biological cliffs. The 1% ER cutoff was set by ASCO/CAP in 2010 and lowered the bar deliberately: it is better to offer endocrine therapy to a patient with marginal benefit than to withhold it from someone who would have responded. The consequence is that "ER-positive" spans a huge range of actual estrogen dependence, and ER-low (1–10%) tumors often behave more like triple-negative disease than like classic luminal cancers.

Note that TNBC is frequently quoted as "10–15%" in the literature; the registry figure is at the bottom of that range.

### Answering the "all positive / double negative / triple negative" question directly

- **All positive (triple positive)** = ER+ **and** PR+ **and** HER2+. 9.3% of cases. This group gets *both* endocrine therapy and HER2-directed therapy — it is the one subtype where the two major targeted pathways are attacked simultaneously. It tends to be less endocrine-sensitive than HR+/HER2− disease because HER2 signaling provides an escape route around ER blockade.

- **Double negative** is not standard clinical terminology, and this is worth being precise about. What people usually mean is one of:
  - **HR−/HER2+** (ER−, PR−, HER2+) — the HER2-enriched subtype. Two receptors negative, one positive.
  - **ER+/PR−/HER2−** — a real and common pattern (~10–15% of HR+ disease), generally more aggressive and less endocrine-responsive than ER+/PR+.
  - **HR+/HER2−** loosely described as "negative for two of three" — but this is the *majority* subtype and is essentially never called double negative.

  If someone says "double negative" without qualification, ask which two.

- **Triple negative (TNBC)** = ER− **and** PR− **and** HER2−. 10.8% of cases. Defined by what it *lacks*, which is exactly the problem: no endocrine target, no HER2 target. Historically chemotherapy-only. Enriched in *BRCA1* carriers, in younger patients, and — in the US — disproportionately affects Black women, who have roughly twice the TNBC incidence of white women and worse stage-adjusted outcomes.

### The edge cases

- **ER+/PR−** — common, real, and prognostically meaningful. Suggests a partly dysfunctional ER pathway.
- **ER−/PR+** — reported in <1% of cases and **most often a testing artifact**. Current ASCO/CAP guidance is to retest; genuine ER−/PR+ biology is doubtful, and these cases usually resolve to ER+ or triple-negative on repeat testing.

## 3. The HER2-low and HER2-ultralow overlay

This is the most important recent change to the taxonomy, and it does **not** create a fifth box in the matrix above. It cuts *across* HR status, subdividing everything currently called "HER2-negative."

| Category | IHC / ISH definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| HER2-positive | IHC 3+, or IHC 2+ / ISH-amplified | Classic HER2+ |
| **HER2-low** | IHC **1+**, or IHC **2+ / ISH-negative** | ~50% of all HER2-negative disease |
| **HER2-ultralow** | IHC **0** with faint, incomplete staining in ≤10% of cells | Introduced via DESTINY-Breast06 |
| HER2-zero | IHC 0 with no staining at all | |

**Why it exists.** It is a *therapy-selection* category invented by a drug, not a biological subtype discovered by pathologists. Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) is an antibody-drug conjugate: the antibody finds HER2, but the cytotoxic payload kills the cell and neighboring cells regardless. That means it works at HER2 expression levels far below the classic amplification threshold. DESTINY-Breast04 proved this for HER2-low; DESTINY-Breast06 extended it to ultralow.

**The honest caveat.** ASCO/CAP's position is that HER2-low is *not* a distinct biological entity with its own prognosis — and the IHC 0 vs 1+ distinction is poorly reproducible between pathologists and unstable across samples from the same patient. A patient can score 0 on one biopsy and 1+ on another. So a category that now gates access to an effective drug rests on a measurement the field admits is unreliable at exactly the boundary that matters. Assays purpose-built for the low range are an active area of work.

## 4. Beyond IHC — molecular and histologic classification

### Intrinsic (PAM50) subtypes

Gene-expression profiling gives a different and partly non-overlapping taxonomy:

| Intrinsic subtype | Rough IHC correlate | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Luminal A** | HR+/HER2−, low Ki-67, high PR | Best prognosis; most endocrine-sensitive; often chemo-sparable |
| **Luminal B** | HR+, higher Ki-67, may be HER2+ | More proliferative; more likely to need chemotherapy |
| **HER2-enriched** | usually HR−/HER2+ | Aggressive; highly responsive to HER2 therapy |
| **Basal-like** | ~80% overlap with TNBC | Aggressive; *BRCA1*-associated; platinum-sensitive |
| **Normal-like** | — | Poorly characterized; may be a sampling artifact |

**The two systems disagree**, and it is now measured rather than asserted. Cross-tabulating PAM50 calls against receptor IHC in the TCGA 2012 cohort (496 classifiable samples): 86% of TNBC is basal-like (76/88) and 83% of basal-like is TNBC (76/92) — but 14 basal-like tumors were HR+/HER2− by IHC.

The HER2 axis diverges far more sharply, and asymmetrically. Every HER2+/HR− tumor was PAM50 HER2-enriched (19/19), yet only **36% of HER2-enriched tumors were HER2+/HR−** (19/53); the remainder split into 18 triple-positive, 11 HR+/HER2− and 5 triple-negative. Treating "HER2-enriched" and "HER2-positive" as synonyms is therefore a material error, not a pedantic one.

Commercial genomic assays (Oncotype DX, MammaPrint, Prosigna) sit alongside this, used mainly to decide whether an HR+/HER2− early-stage patient can safely skip chemotherapy.

### TNBC is not one disease (Lehmann subtypes)

TNBC splits into transcriptional subtypes with different vulnerabilities:

- **BL1** (basal-like 1) — DNA damage response; platinum- and PARP-sensitive
- **BL2** (basal-like 2) — growth factor signaling
- **M** (mesenchymal) — motility, EMT
- **MSL** (mesenchymal stem-like) — low proliferation, angiogenesis *(collapsed into M in later refinements)*
- **IM** (immunomodulatory) — immune-infiltrated; the group most likely to respond to checkpoint inhibitors
- **LAR** (luminal androgen receptor) — AR-driven despite being ER−; a candidate for anti-androgen strategies

This heterogeneity is the main reason TNBC trials have historically underperformed: a drug targeting one subtype gets diluted across a mixed population.

### Histologic types and special situations

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Invasive ductal carcinoma (NST)** | ~70–80% of invasive cases; the default |
| **Invasive lobular carcinoma** | ~10–15%; *CDH1*-loss driven, grows in diffuse single-file strands, often mammographically occult, usually strongly ER+ |
| **DCIS** | Stage 0, non-invasive; receptor status still assessed for endocrine risk reduction |
| **LCIS** | A risk marker rather than a true precursor lesion |
| **Inflammatory breast cancer** | ~1–5%; clinical diagnosis (skin edema, peau d'orange); often HER2+ or TNBC; always at least stage IIIB |
| **Metaplastic** | Rare; usually triple-negative; chemoresistant |
| **Male breast cancer** | ~1% of cases; overwhelmingly ER+ |
| **Paget disease of the nipple** | Usually indicates underlying DCIS or invasive disease |

## 5. Subtype by subtype — standard of care and what's in trials

### 5.1 HR+/HER2− — the largest group (70.1%)

**Current standard.** Early-stage: endocrine therapy for 5–10 years (tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitor), with adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibitors (abemaciclib per monarchE, ribociclib per NATALEE) for high-risk disease. Metastatic: endocrine therapy + a CDK4/6 inhibitor first line, which extends PFS by roughly 10–12 months over ET alone.

**The central clinical problem** is what happens *after* CDK4/6 inhibitor progression. That question is driving nearly the entire HR+ pipeline, and it split into three attack routes:

**Route 1 — better ER blockade (oral SERDs and degraders).** When tumors acquire *ESR1* mutations, the receptor activates without estrogen and aromatase inhibitors stop working. Four agents are now approved here:

- **Elacestrant** (Stemline/Menarini) — first oral SERD, approved 2023, *ESR1*-mutant
- **Imlunestrant** (Lilly, Inluriyo) — approved Sept 2025; EMBER-3 showed PFS HR 0.62 and an ~11.4-month numerical OS advantage
- **Vepdegestrant** (Arvinas, Veppanu) — approved **2026-05-01**; notable as the **first PROTAC protein degrader ever approved by the FDA**, in any disease. ESR1m mPFS 5.0 vs 2.1 months
- **Giredestrant** (Roche) — NDA accepted June 2026 under Priority Review for the *adjuvant* setting, **PDUFA 2026-11-30**. The lidERA trial makes it the first oral SERD with positive phase III data in the curative setting — a much larger commercial opportunity than metastatic ESR1m

And one instructive failure: **camizestrant** (AstraZeneca) tested a genuinely novel idea in SERENA-6 — monitor ctDNA during first-line therapy and switch *before* radiographic progression when an *ESR1* mutation emerges. PFS looked strong (16.0–16.6 vs 9.2 months, HR ~0.44–0.46). But **ODAC voted 6–3 against on 2026-04-30**, citing immature OS, thin patient-reported outcomes, no crossover, and skepticism that ctDNA progression is a clinically meaningful event on its own. The FDA extended its decision date. Meanwhile CHMP recommended approval in Europe and the drug is authorized in Saudi Arabia and the UAE. This is a live regulatory divergence over whether molecular progression is a valid basis to act.

**Route 2 — the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway.** The main resistance pathway downstream of ER:

- **Alpelisib** (Novartis) — PI3Kα, *PIK3CA*-mutant only
- **Capivasertib** (AstraZeneca) — AKT, for *PIK3CA*/*AKT1*/*PTEN*-altered tumors
- **Gedatolisib** (Celcuity, Revtorpyk) — approved **2026-07-14**, and the interesting one: it targets **PIK3CA wild-type** disease — the ~60% of HR+/HER2− patients that every other pathway drug excludes. It inhibits all four class I PI3K isoforms plus both mTOR complexes. In VIKTORIA-1 the triplet and doublet cut progression/death risk by 76% and 67% vs fulvestrant. Celcuity plans a *PIK3CA-mutant* sNDA in Q3 2026, which would take it head-on against alpelisib

  ⚠️ Note the direction carefully — gedatolisib's approved population is **wild-type**, the inverse of alpelisib's. This is easy to get backwards.

- **TER-2013** (Terremoto) — AKT1-selective, FDA Fast Track 2026-06-03, early phase
- **Everolimus** (Novartis, Afinitor) — the original mTOR inhibitor in this space (BOLERO-2, 2012), still used with exemestane after non-steroidal AI failure, though largely displaced by the biomarker-selected agents above

**Route 3 — better CDK inhibition.** Rather than a fourth me-too CDK4/6 inhibitor, the field moved to selectivity:

- **Atirmociclib / PF-07220060** (Pfizer) — **CDK4-selective, CDK6-sparing**. Sparing CDK6 avoids the neutropenia that limits dosing of current agents, allowing deeper CDK4 suppression. Positive phase 2 readouts (FOURLIGHT-1, ReDiscover) in early 2026; now entering multiple phase 3 trials, including post-CDK4/6i patients
- **Culmerciclib / TQB3616** (Chia Tai Tianqing) — CDK2/4/6, positive randomized phase 3 with fulvestrant. CDK2 is implicated in CDK4/6i resistance

**Route 4 — abandon endocrine therapy, use ADCs.** T-DXd (HER2-low/ultralow), sacituzumab govitecan (TROPiCS-02), and Dato-DXd (TROPION-Breast01) are all approved in pretreated HR+/HER2− disease. Coming: **sacituzumab tirumotecan** (Merck/Kelun) in TroFuse-010, 1,200 patients, readout ~2027; and **BNT323/DB-1303** (BioNTech/DualityBio) in DYNASTY-Breast02 for endocrine-resistant HER2-low.

### 5.2 HER2-positive (~15–20% including triple positive)

**Current standard.** Early-stage: neoadjuvant chemotherapy + trastuzumab + pertuzumab (THP), then trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1, Kadcyla) for residual disease (KATHERINE). Metastatic: THP first line, T-DXd second line, then tucatinib-based combinations.

**2026 changed the early-stage standard.** On **2026-05-15** the FDA approved T-DXd (Enhertu) for **two new early-stage indications** — neoadjuvant treatment of HER2+ stage II/III disease followed by THP, and adjuvant treatment of residual invasive disease. DESTINY-Breast11 showed neoadjuvant T-DXd→THP beat anthracycline-based ddAC-THP on pCR *and* had lower rates of severe adverse events. Better and less toxic simultaneously is rare, and it moves the field toward anthracycline-free treatment.

**First-line metastatic is next.** DESTINY-Breast09 reported mPFS **40.7 vs 26.9 months** for T-DXd + pertuzumab vs THP — the first regimen to beat THP in first line. If approved, T-DXd will occupy first line through adjuvant, and the sequencing question becomes what to use *after* T-DXd.

**That "after T-DXd" gap is the emerging battleground.** **Zanidatamab** (Jazz/Zymeworks, Ziihera) is a HER2 biparatopic bispecific being studied specifically in post-T-DXd patients, and in NeoZanHER produced a **30% pCR rate as neoadjuvant monotherapy** — no chemotherapy at all, which points toward de-escalation.

Also in this space: tucatinib (CNS-active, valuable given HER2+ brain metastasis rates), neratinib, lapatinib (largely superseded), margetuximab (limited uptake).

### 5.3 Triple positive (ER+/PR+/HER2+, 9.3%)

Rarely trialed as its own population — usually enrolled inside HER2+ trials with an HR+ subgroup analysis. The distinguishing management question is how to sequence and combine two targeted modalities: dual HER2 blockade plus endocrine therapy, with ET maintenance after chemotherapy stops. Because HER2 signaling drives endocrine resistance, these patients get less benefit from endocrine therapy alone than HR+/HER2− patients. Pfizer is exploring atirmociclib + letrozole specifically in HR+/HER2+ metastatic disease.

### 5.4 Triple-negative (10.8%)

**2026 was the year TNBC first line was rebuilt.** Previously: chemotherapy, plus pembrolizumab if PD-L1 CPS ≥10. Now first line is ADC-based, and the two TROP2 ADCs split the population by PD-L1 status:

| | Approved | Population | Trial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Datopotamab deruxtecan** (Datroway, Daiichi/AZ) | **2026-05-22** | 1L metastatic, **not** PD-1/PD-L1 candidates | TROPION-Breast02 (NCT05374512) — 43% reduction in progression/death; first targeted agent with an OS advantage in this population |
| **Sacituzumab govitecan** (Trodelvy, Gilead) | **2026-06-24** | 1L metastatic, monotherapy **and** with pembrolizumab | ASCENT-03 (38% risk reduction, IO-ineligible) and ASCENT-04 (mPFS 11.2 vs 7.8 mo, PD-L1+) |

Both landed within five weeks of each other and overlap heavily in the IO-ineligible segment — a genuine head-to-head commercial fight with no comparative trial to settle it.

**Early-stage** remains KEYNOTE-522 (neoadjuvant chemo + pembrolizumab, then adjuvant pembrolizumab), now with 7-year EFS and OS confirmation. Two directions are being tested at once:
- **De-escalation** — a phase 3 comparing adjuvant pembrolizumab vs observation in patients who already achieved pCR, asking whether a year of immunotherapy and its permanent endocrinopathy risk can be dropped
- **Escalation** — OptimICE-RD/ASCENT-05 (SG + pembrolizumab for residual disease), TROPION-Breast04 (Dato-DXd replacing the chemo backbone entirely), SCARLET (carboplatin + IO)

**The unsolved problem is biomarkers.** PD-L1 CPS is a mediocre predictor, TROP2 expression does not predict TROP2-ADC benefit, and the Lehmann subtypes have never been translated into a usable clinical assay. Patient selection in TNBC lags well behind the drugs.

Earlier-stage assets — leronlimab (CytoDyn, CCR5), eganelisib (Infinity, PI3Kγ), MDNA11 (Medicenna, IL-2 superkine), CFI-400945 (Treadwell, PLK4) — remain phase 1/2 with thin data.

**A cautionary note:** atezolizumab (Roche) was granted accelerated approval in TNBC on IMpassion130, then **voluntarily withdrawn in the US in 2021** when IMpassion131 failed to confirm benefit. It is in `pipeline.csv` as `discontinued` on purpose — a landscape that shows only winners misleads.

### 5.5 HER2-low / HER2-ultralow (cuts across ~50% of HER2-negative disease)

T-DXd owns this category outright today via DESTINY-Breast04 (HER2-low) and DESTINY-Breast06 (extending to ultralow and moving earlier, before chemotherapy). Challengers are BNT323/DB-1303 (BioNTech/DualityBio, DYNASTY-Breast02) and, indirectly, the TROP2 ADCs, which do not care about HER2 status at all — arguably the strongest competitive threat, since they sidestep the unreliable IHC 0-vs-1+ call entirely.

## 6. Company landscape — organized by contested target class

Competition here runs by target class, not by company. Grouping this way shows where the actual fights are.

### TROP2 ADCs — the most contested space in breast cancer

| Company | Asset | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Gilead** | Sacituzumab govitecan (Trodelvy) | First mover (2020). Now 1L TNBC mono + with pembrolizumab, plus HR+/HER2− |
| **Daiichi Sankyo / AstraZeneca** | Datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway) | 1L TNBC in IO-ineligible patients; HR+/HER2−; pushing into neoadjuvant |
| **Merck / Kelun** | Sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT) | Fast follower, phase 3 TroFuse-010, readout ~2027 |

Three TROP2 ADCs against the same antigen, differentiated mainly by payload and linker chemistry rather than target biology — which is also why their toxicity profiles differ meaningfully.

### HER2 ADCs

**Daiichi Sankyo/AstraZeneca dominate.** T-DXd now spans 2L metastatic HER2+, neoadjuvant and adjuvant early-stage HER2+, HER2-low, and HER2-ultralow, with 1L metastatic pending on DESTINY-Breast09. It is the most successful ADC franchise in oncology. Roche holds the older generation (trastuzumab, pertuzumab, T-DM1) and is being displaced from settings it defined. **BioNTech/DualityBio** (BNT323) is the credible challenger, part of a broader wave of China-originated ADCs licensed into Western development.

### Oral SERDs and ER degraders

**Lilly** (imlunestrant) and **Arvinas** (vepdegestrant) are approved in ESR1m metastatic; **Stemline/Menarini** (elacestrant) got there first. **Roche** (giredestrant) is playing for the much bigger prize — the adjuvant early-stage setting, PDUFA 2026-11-30. **AstraZeneca** (camizestrant) has the strongest PFS data of the group and the worst regulatory position, having lost an ODAC vote in the US while succeeding in the EU. Arvinas' approval also validates targeted protein degradation as a modality far beyond this one indication.

### PI3K / AKT / mTOR

**Novartis** (alpelisib) and **AstraZeneca** (capivasertib) hold the biomarker-selected mutant populations. **Celcuity**, a small company, took the segment everyone else skipped — **PIK3CA wild-type**, ~60% of HR+/HER2− patients — and got there first with gedatolisib. Its Q3 2026 sNDA for the mutant population would put it in direct competition with Novartis.

### CDK inhibition

**Pfizer** (palbociclib), **Novartis** (ribociclib), and **Lilly** (abemaciclib) hold a mature market where ribociclib and abemaciclib have pulled ahead on survival and adjuvant data. The next generation is about selectivity: **Pfizer's atirmociclib** (CDK4-selective, CDK6-sparing) is the lead asset and a rare case of a company credibly cannibalizing its own franchise. **Chia Tai Tianqing's culmerciclib** (CDK2/4/6) attacks resistance from the opposite direction by adding CDK2.

### Immuno-oncology in TNBC

**Merck** owns this outright with pembrolizumab — KEYNOTE-522 in early-stage and KEYNOTE-355 in PD-L1+ metastatic — and has now made itself the partner of choice via the ASCENT-04 combination with Gilead's Trodelvy. **Roche** exited after atezolizumab's withdrawal. The open question is whether Merck's franchise gets de-escalated out of the pCR population by its own trial.

### PARP inhibitors

**AstraZeneca/Merck** (olaparib) and **Pfizer** (talazoparib) serve germline *BRCA1/2* carriers across both HR+ and TNBC. A stable, biomarker-defined niche; olaparib's adjuvant OlympiA indication is the more valuable one.

### Bispecifics and next modalities

**Jazz/Zymeworks** (zanidatamab) leads the HER2 bispecific effort, positioned for post-T-DXd and for chemotherapy-free neoadjuvant de-escalation. Cell therapy and therapeutic vaccines remain early in breast cancer with no phase 3 assets — solid tumors continue to resist the approaches that worked in hematologic malignancy.

2. **This is a snapshot.** The pipeline layer was verified 2026-07-26. Six FDA actions in breast cancer occurred between May and July 2026 alone. Treat anything more than a few months old as stale.
3. **The receptor-subtype shares are US registry data; other percentages are not.** Histology and intrinsic-subtype frequencies in this report remain literature estimates and vary by population, ancestry, and screening intensity. Do not read them with the same confidence as the SEER table.
4. **Not medical advice.** Actual treatment depends on stage, prior therapy, genomics, comorbidities, and patient preference.
5. **Confidence varies by row.** Approved agents and phase 3 trials are verified against FDA announcements, company releases, or ClinicalTrials.gov. Early-phase assets (particularly the phase 1/2 TNBC entries) come from secondary market-research sources and are lower confidence — flagged in `SOURCES.md`. Blank fields in `pipeline.csv` mean *not confirmed*, deliberately left empty rather than guessed.
